description: Use when asked how to protect yourself, your home or your family while it is smoky, or how to keep smoke out, or for the best way to protect against wildfire smoke.
---
Source: "Reduce Smoke Exposure Indoors" (EPA fact sheet, 3 pages). Every step ends with its citation: copy it exactly. Keep the steps that fit the user's situation and keep the warnings.

Indoors
1. Stay inside with doors and windows closed. Close the fresh air intake if your cooling or ventilation system has one (sometimes called "recirculate mode"). (Reduce Smoke Exposure Indoors, p. 1)
2. Central air conditioning or a forced air furnace: use high-efficiency filters (MERV 13, or as high as your system can handle) and run the fan continuously. Ask an HVAC professional which filter fits. (Reduce Smoke Exposure Indoors, p. 1)
3. Use a portable air cleaner (air purifier) that is powerful enough for the room size and makes little or no ozone, or a DIY one (box fan plus high-efficiency HVAC filters). Replace filters when dirty, which may be more often than the maker recommends. (Reduce Smoke Exposure Indoors, p. 1)
4. Create a "cleaner air room" for everyone in the household, with a portable air cleaner in it. (Reduce Smoke Exposure Indoors, p. 2)
5. Do not add to indoor pollution: no candles, incense, gas, propane or wood-burning stoves, fireplaces or aerosol sprays; no smoking or vaping; no vacuuming unless the vacuum has a HEPA filter; avoid cooking on a stove or in an oven, especially at high heat or for a long time. (Reduce Smoke Exposure Indoors, p. 2)
6. No air conditioning and too hot: open windows to cool the home, then close doors and windows once it is cool and use an air cleaner. Seek shelter elsewhere if you cannot tolerate smoke and cannot stay cool with the windows closed. (Reduce Smoke Exposure Indoors, p. 2)
7. Consider an air sensor to see which actions reduce indoor smoke. (Reduce Smoke Exposure Indoors, p. 2)
8. Air out your home when air quality improves; long smoke events usually have better periods. (Reduce Smoke Exposure Indoors, p. 2)

Outdoors and on the road
9. Know your air quality: check your state, local or Tribal air quality website or the AirNow Fire and Smoke Map. (Reduce Smoke Exposure Indoors, p. 1)
10. Take it easier: avoid strenuous activities such as mowing the lawn or going for a run. Smoke levels change a lot through the day, so wait for better air. (Reduce Smoke Exposure Indoors, p. 2)
11. Have enough food and medication for several days so you do not have to go out; if you must go out, avoid the smokiest times of day. (Reduce Smoke Exposure Indoors, p. 2)
12. In a vehicle: close the windows and run the air conditioner in recirculate mode; slow down when smoke limits visibility. (Reduce Smoke Exposure Indoors, p. 2)
13. Have NIOSH Approved N95 respirators and learn to use them. Do not rely on dust masks or bandanas. An N95 helps only if it fits snugly with no gaps. They do not come in children's sizes, but some older children may use an adult size small. Children under 2 years old should not wear a respirator. (Reduce Smoke Exposure Indoors, p. 2)

Reduce your risk
14. Pay attention to local air quality reports, health warnings and alerts: they tell you about changing conditions, cancelled events or evacuation notices. (Reduce Smoke Exposure Indoors, p. 3)
15. Have enough medication and food for at least 5 days. Follow your health care provider's advice if you have heart or lung disease; with asthma, follow your asthma management plan. If you feel sick, reduce your exposure and contact your health care provider. (Reduce Smoke Exposure Indoors, p. 3)

For a question about only one part (for example central air), give only the matching steps.
