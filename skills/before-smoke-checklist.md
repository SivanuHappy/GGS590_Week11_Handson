description: Use when asked how to get ready for wildfire smoke, or what to buy or do before smoke arrives.

Source: "Prepare for Wildfire Smoke" (EPA fact sheet, 2 pages). Every step ends with its citation: copy it exactly. Keep the steps that fit the user's situation and keep the warnings.

1. Know how to check your air quality: bookmark your state, local or Tribal air quality website or the Fire and Smoke Map; on AirNow.gov you can sign up for email notifications. (Prepare for Wildfire Smoke, p. 1)
2. Talk with your health care provider about what to do when it is smoky, especially if you are in an at-risk group: people with heart or lung disease, children, older adults, pregnant women, people with greater exposures. With heart or lung disease, make a plan with your provider. (Prepare for Wildfire Smoke, p. 1)
3. Buy a portable air cleaner and replacement filters before smoke arrives. It should be powerful enough for the room size and produce little or no ozone, which is a harmful air pollutant. A DIY air cleaner (box fan plus high-efficiency HVAC filters) is an option. (Prepare for Wildfire Smoke, p. 1)
4. If you have a home HVAC system: ask an HVAC professional which high-efficiency filters to use and how to close the fresh air intake; learn to run the fan continuously; stock compatible filters. (Prepare for Wildfire Smoke, p. 1)
5. Set up a "cleaner air room": big enough for everyone in the household to be comfortable; a bedroom with an attached bathroom is a good choice; have a portable air cleaner ready for it. (Prepare for Wildfire Smoke, p. 1)
6. Know how you will stay cool when it is hot and smoky: heat and smoke together may be much worse for your health than either alone. (Prepare for Wildfire Smoke, p. 2)
7. Stock up so you do not have to go out: several days of food and medications. Have food that needs no cooking or refrigeration in case you lose power, and food that needs no high heat or long cooking, because a stove or oven adds indoor pollution. (Prepare for Wildfire Smoke, p. 2)
8. Have a supply of NIOSH Approved N95 respirators and learn how to use them. Do not rely on dust masks or bandanas for protection from smoke. (Prepare for Wildfire Smoke, p. 2)
9. Plan for pets and large animals: keep pets indoors as much as possible, limit their physical activity, and make sure water is available for all your animals. (Prepare for Wildfire Smoke, p. 2)
10. Buy and learn to use an air sensor to monitor air quality outdoors and indoors. (Prepare for Wildfire Smoke, p. 2)
11. Be able to recognize signs of stress and know how to reduce its impact on you and your family. (Prepare for Wildfire Smoke, p. 2)
