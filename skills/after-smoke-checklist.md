---
name: after-smoke-checklist
description: Use when asked about going back to a home or property after a wildfire, cleaning up ash, or protecting family and pets from ash.
---
Source: "Protect Yourself from Ash" (EPA fact sheet, 3 pages). Every step ends with its citation: copy it exactly. Keep the steps that fit the user's situation and keep the warnings.

Before you go
1. Return only when authorities say it is safe. Ask local authorities whether it is safe to clean up your property and whether they have restrictions. (Protect Yourself from Ash, pp. 1-2)
2. People with heart or lung disease, older adults and pregnant women should not take part in ash cleanup. Children should not be near ash or ash-contaminated sites until the areas are cleaned up; clean ash off children's toys before use. Clean ash off pets and keep them away from contaminated sites. (Protect Yourself from Ash, p. 1)

Protect yourself
3. Lungs: wear a close-fitting NIOSH Approved respirator marked N95 or P100, tightened with a snug seal. Surgical masks and one-strap dust masks do not protect your lungs. With heart or lung disease, talk to your health provider first. (Protect Yourself from Ash, p. 2)
4. Skin and eyes: work gloves, long sleeves, long pants, socks, sturdy thick-soled shoes, goggles and a head covering. Wet ash can cause chemical burns or skin irritation. If ash gets on skin, in eyes or mouth, wash it off as soon as you can. (Protect Yourself from Ash, p. 1)

Cleaning
5. Do not stir up ash: avoid dry sweeping and dusting, and never use a leaf blower. Mist hard surfaces lightly with water before sweeping, then wet mop; use as little water as you can. (Protect Yourself from Ash, p. 2)
6. Thick deposits or materials that absorb water: dry methods such as vacuuming, sticky rollers or "soot" sponges may work. Light deposits: damp wiping or mopping. Use a HEPA vacuum if available; with an ordinary vacuum wear a respirator, air out the space and use an air cleaner. (Protect Yourself from Ash, p. 2)
7. Indoors: open windows and doors if conditions permit, use fans to move air outdoors, run an air cleaner; use the HVAC system only if it is safe, with a high-efficiency filter. (Protect Yourself from Ash, p. 2)
8. Possessions: hard-surface items and washable items can be cleaned; lightly soiled upholstered furniture may be cleanable. Consider professional help for structural damage, hazardous materials, HVAC damage or more than you can manage. (Protect Yourself from Ash, p. 2)

Afterwards
9. Do not bring ash back: wash your hands and change into clean clothes and shoes before getting into your car; seal contaminated clothing and shoes in a plastic bag and put it in the trunk. (Protect Yourself from Ash, p. 1)
10. Food and water: wash fruit and vegetables from trees or gardens where ash fell; do not eat at the site unless the food is in a sealed container; wash hands well before eating; check with your drinking water provider. (Protect Yourself from Ash, pp. 2-3)
11. Disposal: collected ash may go in regular trash, stored in plastic bags or other containers so it is not stirred up. If you suspect hazardous waste such as asbestos, contact your local hazardous waste authorities. Do not wash ash into storm drains. (Protect Yourself from Ash, p. 3)
12. Other dangers: lithium-ion batteries, mold, and asbestos, heavy metals and other chemicals. Do not use ozone or hydroxyl generators or other oxidizers to remove smoke odors. (Protect Yourself from Ash, p. 3)
